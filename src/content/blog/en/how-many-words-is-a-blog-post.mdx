Word count is one of the most searched writing questions on the web. The honest answer: **as long as it needs to be, and not a word longer.** But there are useful ranges.

## Quick reference

| Content type | Typical length |
| --- | --- |
| Tweet / X post | 20–40 words |
| Meta description | 140–160 characters |
| Product page | 300–800 words |
| Standard blog post | 1,000–1,500 words |
| Long-form guide | 2,500+ words |

## Length follows intent

Someone searching *"convert PDF to Word"* wants a tool, not an essay. Someone searching *"how to write a cover letter"* wants depth. Match the searcher's intent first; the word count will follow.

> Write for the reader. Measure for the editor.

## Use the numbers to edit, not to pad

1. Write the draft without looking at the count.
2. Paste it into the counter and check reading time.
3. Cut anything that doesn't earn its place.

<Callout>Tip: aim for a reading time under 7 minutes for most blog posts — engagement drops sharply after that.</Callout>
